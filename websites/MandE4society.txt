irish 2¬The Catholic Irish worked with Protestant Irish over their common cause of teetotalism
irish 3¬The Irish set up Catholic social agencies such as ‘the League of the Cross’ to help combat alcohol use
irish 4¬The Catholic Irish developed a separate Catholic school system in most Scottish cities
irish 5¬The Catholic Irish helped the development of football clubs
irish 6¬Catholic Irish immigrants tended to live in slums, where overcrowding and poor conditions led to the rapid spread of disease such as TB, typhus and Cholera
irish 7¬Catholic Irish tended to live in poorer slum areas of cities where the rent was cheaper
irish 8¬Irish Catholics gradually joined Trade Unions, e.g. they accounted for 3/4 of Lanarkshire Miners’ Union membership by 1900
irish 9¬The Protestant Irish set up Orange Orders across Scotland which became social hubs
jewish 1¬Jewish immigrants tended to be more isolated in their own communities. They initially lived in areas of deprivation such as the Gorbals working long hours in sweated industries
jewish 2¬ewish immigrants looked after their own, setting up The Hebrew Benevolent Loan Society and the Jewish Board of Guardians to provide loans and financial support for poor Jews
jewish 3¬As the Jewish community grew wealthier, they moved out of the Gorbals and into more fashionable places such as the West End of Glasgow or wealthier suburbs
lithuanian 1¬Catholic Lithuanian immigrants, initially active in existing Catholic Churches, later established their own churches
lithuanian 2¬During the First World War the Anglo-Russian agreement resulted in 1100 Lithuanian men being deported to Russia. The families left behind were later denied any benefits and many left the country
italian 1¬Italians opened their shops on a Sunday which challenged Scottish social norms and led to significant opposition from the Presbyterian Church
italian 2¬Disapproval of the fact that young men and women could socialise together unchaperoned in Italian cafés opened these businesses
italian 3¬Italians lived together in their own separate communities, working with family members, speaking Italian and marrying their own kind
italian 4¬During the inter-war period, the Italians set up their own groups: La Casa del Fascio
